Claim Block

The single claim that, if false, makes this work worthless: a monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenide on a silicon nitride waveguide, doped by protons from a sealed solid-state reservoir, holds its optical phase shift at open circuit while keeping |Δn/Δk| well above 10 at 1550 nm.

Who can check it: no literature source answers it; only the decisive experiment can. The supporting gap claim (that no proton-programmed waveguide weight has been published) can be checked in the literature, but only to the extent of the search scope stated in the abstract.

Abstract

WIP · draft 2 · pending human archive pass

Storing neural-network weights directly in the optical layer of a photonic accelerator would remove the repeated weight fetch from electronic memory. Three families of non-volatile photonic weight have been demonstrated on integrated waveguides: phase-change materials; ferroelectric barium titanate, which provides analogue, non-volatile phase tuning with no absorption changeV1; and copper-ion ferroionic two-dimensional heterostructures on silicon nitride and silicon microrings, one of which reports 8-bit multilevel storage with projected retention beyond ten yearsV2.

Proton programming of optical properties is established outside integrated photonics. A thin-film solid-state proton pump retains its optical state at open circuitV3, and room-temperature, all-solid-state protonation switches the infrared transmittance of vanadium dioxide in a non-volatile mannerV4. In a targeted literature search covering English-language sources and regional passes on Korea, Japan, China and Europe, we did not find a waveguide-integrated optical weight programmed by protons. The same search suggests why a direct approach is unpromising: in the two hosts examined, hydrogenated tungsten oxide and hydrogenated perovskite nickelates, proton loading is reported to change absorption alongside the refractive index, although the nickelate data located so far are at long-wave infrared rather than 1550 nmV5.

We therefore propose a split architecture in which protons store the state and a separate low-loss layer carries the optics: a sealed solid proton reservoir; a single-layer hexagonal boron nitride membrane, a structure reported to improve retention in proton-based electrochemical memoryV6; and a monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenide on a silicon nitride microring, a platform in which ionic-liquid gating of monolayer WS2 produced |Δn/Δk| ≈ 125 with negligible insertion lossV7. We pre-register kill conditions and define one decisive experiment: measuring Δn/Δk, open-circuit retention and write speed for a single proton-gated monolayer on one ring. Because copper-ion devices already combine low loss, multilevel storage and long projected retention, a proton-programmed weight is worth pursuing only if it offers a distinct advantage, most plausibly front-end fabrication compatibility, without losing on optical loss, write speed or retention.

Tombstones

Claims made during drafting that were withdrawn or narrowed. They stay visible rather than being silently corrected.

T1 · narrowed

A 2026 review characterizes near-infrared electrochemical modulation in general as amplitude-dominant. The source sentence describes one device class (electrochemical doping near an epsilon-near-zero condition), not the field. Replaced by the host-specific claim in V5.

T2 · withdrawn

No one has combined ionic storage with a low-loss 2D optical layer on a waveguide. Falsified by V2. The surviving gap is protons specifically.

T3 · excluded

Monolayer WS2 shows Δn = 0.53 with Δk = 0.004. Seen only in a secondary review and not confirmed in the primary paper. Only the primary abstract value is used.

T4 · withdrawn

Copper-ion ferroionic weights write slowly, so protons would win on write speed. The slow resonance shift belongs to one lateral-migration microring device (Tamalampudi et al.). The vertical heterostructure of Singh et al. is not slow in the same way, so write speed is no longer claimed as the proton advantage.
